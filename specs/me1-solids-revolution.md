# Solids of revolution

> Backlog summary: Spin a region about either axis, show disc slices and the volume integral.

## Learning goal
Students see a solid of revolution being generated by spinning a plane region about the x-axis or the y-axis, and understand the volume formula V = π∫ y² dx (or π∫ x² dy) as the limit of a sum of thin discs of volume πr²Δx. They compare a disc sum with the exact integral and watch the error shrink as the discs get thinner.

## What the student sees
Default view at 1920×1080 (projector theme), y = √x on 0 ≤ x ≤ 4 about the x-axis, 10 discs at the midpoint, perspective camera looking from the front-left and slightly above:

- **3D view (left, about 65% of the stage):** labelled x, y and z axes. The plane region under the curve (between the curve, the axis of rotation and the bounds) is a flat orange-outlined shape in the xy-plane. The solid is the lathe surface of the curve, drawn as a matte light grey mesh with fine grid lines (no shine, no gradient) swept through the angle set by "Revolve by" (0° shows only the region; 360° the full solid). The discs (cylinders of radius |f| at the chosen sample point and thickness Δ) are drawn as flat orange slabs with dark edges, inside a semi-transparent surface.
- **Integral panel (right):** in KaTeX, V = π∫₀⁴ (√x)² dx = π∫₀⁴ x dx = 8π, followed by the disc sum Σ π rᵢ² Δx with the current number of discs, and the error. A small 2D inset shows the curve and the region with the disc sample points marked.
- Dragging rotates the 3D view (OrbitControls); "Reset view" restores it. The "Revolve by" slider spins the region round the axis.

How the engine computes (static setup, `meta.staticSetups` includes `revolution`):
- **volume** = π∫ f² over [lower, upper] by adaptive Simpson's rule to a relative tolerance of 1e-10.
- **discSum** = Σ π f(sᵢ)² Δ for i = 0 … N − 1, where Δ = (upper − lower)/N and the sample point is sᵢ = lower + (upper − lower)·j/N with j = i (left), i + ½ (midpoint) or i + 1 (right), computed from j directly (never by repeated addition) and then clamped to [lower, upper]. This way the last right-rule disc lands exactly on the upper bound, never one rounding step past it (which would make √(9 − x²) undefined at x = 3).
- If f is undefined at any sample point (for example √ of a negative number), the stage says "the curve is undefined at …" and the outputs are NaN.

## Presets
| Name | Values | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| default | f = `sqrt(x)`, axis x, 0 to 4, 10 discs, midpoint | A paraboloid: V = 8π, and midpoint discs are exact here because f² = x is linear |
| cone | f = `x`, axis x, 0 to 1, 10 discs, midpoint | The cone V = π/3 |
| sphere | f = `sqrt(9 - x^2)`, axis x, −3 to 3, 10 discs, midpoint | A sphere of radius 3: V = 36π = (4/3)π(3³) |
| power | f = `x^2`, axis x, 0 to 2, 4 discs, left | Coarse left discs underestimate badly; raise the number of discs |
| bowl-y | f = `sqrt(y)`, axis y, 0 to 4, 10 discs, midpoint | y = x² spun about the y-axis: V = π∫ y dy = 8π |
| sphere-y | f = `sqrt(4 - y^2)`, axis y, −2 to 2, 20 discs, midpoint | A sphere about the y-axis: V = 32π/3 |

## Assumptions shown on stage
- The region lies between the curve and the axis of rotation
- Volume from numerical integration (adaptive Simpson's rule)
- Lengths in units; volumes in cubic units

## Try this (3–5 predict, observe, explain prompts)
1. Predict: in the power preset (y = x² on [0, 2], 4 left discs), will the disc sum be more or less than the true volume? By how much, roughly?
2. Observe: switch the power preset from left to right discs, then to midpoint. Which is closest? Now raise the number of discs to 100.
3. Predict: in the default preset, the midpoint disc sum equals the integral exactly. Change to the cone preset: is it still exact? Why not?
4. Observe: in the sphere preset, set "Revolve by" to 90°, then 180°, then 360°. What fraction of the sphere's volume is drawn at each stage?
5. Explain: in bowl-y, why does the formula use x² dy instead of y² dx, and why does f need to be written in terms of y?

## Teacher notes outline
- Misconceptions to target: V = π∫ y dx (forgetting to square); V = ∫ πy² dx about the y-axis (using the wrong variable); a disc's volume is πrΔx with r not squared; more discs always change the answer (midpoint discs can be exact when f² is linear); rotating about the y-axis requires the curve as x in terms of y.
- 10-minute lesson flow: 0–2 min sweep the default region from 0° to 360°; 2–4 min show one disc and write its volume πr²Δx; 4–6 min the power preset with 4, 20, 100 discs; 6–8 min the integral panel and the cone and sphere formulas; 8–10 min bowl-y and the change of variable.
- Original HSC-style question idea: the region bounded by y = eˣ, the x-axis, x = 0 and x = ln 3 is rotated about the x-axis. (a) Show that the volume of the solid formed is 4π cubic units. (b) The same region is cut into n discs of equal thickness, with each radius taken at the left end of its slice. Explain, without calculating, whether the disc sum is greater or less than 4π. Full question and worked solution go in the notes file.

## Accessibility notes
- Second cues beyond colour: the discs have dark outlines and are opaque, the surface is a light mesh; the region in the plane has a solid outline; the axis of rotation is drawn thicker and labelled "axis of rotation".
- Keyboard: all parameters are panel controls; the camera turns with the arrow keys when the stage has focus; "Reset view" restores it.
- Stage aria-label (from `describe()`), for example: "Solid formed by rotating y = √x for x from 0 to 4 about the x-axis, shown with 10 midpoint discs. Volume 8π, about 25.13 cubic units; disc sum 25.13."

## Engine change requests

- Outputs: `volume`, `volumeOverPi`, `discSum`, `discError`, `discWidth`.

## Acceptance
Every number below comes from the formula and hand calculation shown here. The integral uses relative tolerance 1e-8 (numerical integration); disc sums are finite sums computed in closed form here (relative 1e-9).

- **Check 1, cone with 200 midpoint discs.** f(x) = x on [0, 1]: V = π∫₀¹ x² dx = **π/3 = 1.047197551**; V/π = **0.3333333333**. Δ = 1/200 = **0.005**. Midpoint sum: π Σ ((i + ½)/200)² × (1/200) = (π/200³) Σ (i + ½)² for i = 0 … 199. Using Σ (i + ½)² = N(4N² − 1)/12 with N = 200: the sum is π(4N² − 1)/(12N²) = π/3 − π/(12 × 40 000) = 1.047197551 − 0.000006545 = **1.047191006**.
- **Check 2, default paraboloid.** f = √x on [0, 4]: V = π∫₀⁴ x dx = π × 8 = **8π = 25.13274123**; V/π = **8**. Midpoint, 10 discs (Δ = 0.4, midpoints 0.2, 0.6, …, 3.8): f² = x is linear, so the midpoint sum is exact: π × 0.4 × (0.2 + 0.6 + … + 3.8) = π × 0.4 × 20 = **8π**, error **0**.
- **Check 3, the same with left and right discs.** Left points 0, 0.4, …, 3.6: π × 0.4 × (0 + 0.4 + … + 3.6) = π × 0.4 × 18 = **7.2π = 22.61946711**. Right points 0.4, …, 4: π × 0.4 × 22 = **8.8π = 27.64601535**.
- **Check 4, power preset.** f = x² on [0, 2]: V = π∫₀² x⁴ dx = π × 32/5 = **6.4π = 20.10619298**. Four left discs (Δ = 0.5, points 0, 0.5, 1, 1.5): π × 0.5 × (0 + 0.0625 + 1 + 5.0625) = π × 0.5 × 6.125 = **3.0625π = 9.621127502**. Four midpoint discs (0.25, 0.75, 1.25, 1.75): x⁴ = 0.00390625, 0.31640625, 2.44140625, 9.37890625, sum 12.140625, so π × 0.5 × 12.140625 = **6.0703125π = 19.07044915**.
- **Check 5, sphere of radius 3 about the x-axis.** f² = 9 − x² on [−3, 3]: V = π[9x − x³/3] from −3 to 3 = π(18 + 18) = **36π = 113.0973355** (= (4/3)π × 27). Ten midpoint discs: Δ = 0.6, midpoints ±0.3, ±0.9, ±1.5, ±2.1, ±2.7; Σ m² = 2(0.09 + 0.81 + 2.25 + 4.41 + 7.29) = 29.7, so Σ (9 − m²) = 90 − 29.7 = 60.3 and the disc sum is π × 0.6 × 60.3 = **36.18π = 113.6628222**; error **0.18π = 0.5654866776**.
- **Check 6, bowl about the y-axis.** x = √y on [0, 4] about the y-axis: V = π∫₀⁴ y dy = **8π = 25.13274123**.
- **Check 7, sphere of radius 2 about the y-axis.** x² = 4 − y² on [−2, 2]: V = π(16 − 16/3) = **32π/3 = 33.51032164**. Twenty midpoint discs: Δ = 0.2, midpoints ±0.1, ±0.3, …, ±1.9; Σ m² = 2 × 0.01 × (1² + 3² + … + 19²) = 0.02 × 1330 = 26.6, so Σ (4 − m²) = 80 − 26.6 = 53.4 and the disc sum is π × 0.2 × 53.4 = **10.68π = 33.55220954**.
