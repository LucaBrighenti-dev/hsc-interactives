## Misconceptions

- **"A bigger amplitude means a longer period."** The period depends only on the frequency: $T = \frac{1}{f}$. Change $A$ with $f$ fixed and the period readout does not move.
- **"Phase moves the graph up."** The phase $\phi$ shifts the graph sideways in time. The vertical position is set by $A$ and the sine itself.
- **"The phasor is a real object going round."** The phasor is a picture of the angle $2\pi f t + \phi$. Its vertical projection is the displacement.

## 10-minute lesson flow

1. **(2 min) Predict.** Show the standard wave paused at $t = 0$. Ask the class to predict the period from $f = 0.5$ Hz.
2. **(3 min) Observe.** Play. Read the period readout and compare it with the predictions. Step forward with the `.` key to stop at the first crest.
3. **(3 min) Change one thing.** Double $f$, then double $A$. Students record which readouts change.
4. **(2 min) Explain.** Set $\phi = 90°$ and ask why the graph now starts at its maximum.

## Try this: answers

1. Doubling $f$ halves the period, because $T = \frac{1}{f}$: from 2 s to 1 s.
2. With $\phi = 90°$, $y(0) = A\sin 90° = A$, so the graph starts at the crest and the phasor points straight up.
3. The phasor tip's height is $A\sin(2\pi f t + \phi)$, which is the displacement $y$ at that instant, so the connector is horizontal.

## Original HSC-style question

A point moves so that its displacement is $y = 3\sin\left(4\pi t + \frac{\pi}{6}\right)$ metres, where $t$ is in seconds.

(a) Find the period of the motion. (1 mark)

(b) Find the first time $t > 0$ at which $y = 3$. (2 marks)

### Worked solution

(a) Here $2\pi f = 4\pi$, so $f = 2$ Hz and $T = \frac{1}{f} = 0.5$ s.

(b) $y = 3$ when $\sin\left(4\pi t + \frac{\pi}{6}\right) = 1$, so $4\pi t + \frac{\pi}{6} = \frac{\pi}{2}$, giving $4\pi t = \frac{\pi}{3}$ and $t = \frac{1}{12}$ s $\approx 0.0833$ s.

## Assumptions and limits

- An ideal sine wave: no damping, so the amplitude never decreases.
- The phase is entered in degrees and measured anticlockwise from the positive horizontal axis of the phasor.
- The simulation advances in fixed steps of $\frac{1}{240}$ s; readouts are exact values of the formula at those times.
